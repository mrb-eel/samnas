# HOLD MY PLACE: story bible

This is the author's knowledge. The player learns it slowly and some of it never.
Everything in the script (`game/story/*.hmp`) should agree with this file. If the
script and this file disagree, fix one of them on purpose.

---

## 1. The place

**Ferrier Court** is a six-storey council block of 42 flats, built in 1968 on top of
the **Ferrier Street Municipal Baths** (opened 1934). The pool closed in 1988 and was
drained. The pool hall is still there under the ground floor, reached through the old
lifeguard office (**G/1**) and the plant rooms.

The city is never named. The register is British: flats, the council, buses, a
fortnight, a kettle.

**The exchange.** The Baths had an internal switchboard from the 1950s. In 1968 it
was extended to serve the flats (extensions 200 to 260). It runs the door release,
the lift alarm, free internal calls, and one external line group. The council
forgot it existed around 2004. **Inez Carvalho** kept it working as the building's
maintenance electrician from 1996 and has kept it working unpaid since she retired
in 2019. Parts of it are held together with residents' things: labels cut off food
packets used as cable tags, a hair clip (Mrs. Kaye's) round a loom, a pink ribbon
(Tobi's) tied on the line for 3B "so we know which is ours", and a plaster figure
of the Virgin in the gap where a board was lost in the 2009 flood.

Useful extensions:

| Ext | What |
|---|---|
| 200 | Front door panel. Dial 9 while connected to release the door. |
| 201 | Receiving Room handset. |
| 210 | Overnight Support Service desk (lobby). |
| 247 | G/1, the old lifeguard office. Unused until six weeks ago. **Ari's line.** |

The building's external number is **01632 960 200**. Six weeks ago Inez routed a
second external number, **01632 960 247**, to extension 247 and patched an old
answering-machine board onto it, because Jad asked her to.

**The Receiving Room** is behind the deep end, through the plant room. White tiles,
a shallow porcelain basin like a reclining bath (the residents' word is "the
cradle"), a wall handset on ext 201, a brass token meter that opens the warm feed
from the old boiler, anatomical diagrams on the wall, framed photographs, and the
**Receiving Register** (1951 to 1979). About 140 arrivals were received here.
Receiving was "centralised" to the Civic Centre in 1979, which stopped doing it
in 2004. Arrivals are now handled on paper by **Community Services: Arrivals &
Registration**, a desk that since the last round of cuts is the same team that
runs the Designated Personal Contact scheme. A handful of old rooms still work.
This is one.

**The Overnight Support Service** has run a staffed desk in the Ferrier Court lobby
from 20:00 to 06:00 since 1991. It answers the building's emergency line, sorts
lockouts, checks on people, and books escorts to appointments. It closes at
**06:00 on Saturday 26 September**, the morning the game ends.

## 2. The paperwork

- **DPC-1, Designated Personal Contact nomination.** Every resident must name a
  contact who will "answer calls, accompany you to appointments, and respond in an
  emergency". When every resident has one, the overnight service is judged
  unnecessary. The council's guidance leaflet had a filled-in example: *Contact
  name: ARI (SAMPLE). Relationship: Friend.* Jad reformatted the council's
  two-page PDF so it printed on one sheet. Anyone who has seen both can tell which
  one a form was printed from.
- **The system.** When a nominated contact has no national record, the council's
  software flags them *Unregistered: possible arrival*, because that is the only
  workflow it has for a person with no record. When five or more residents of one
  building name the same contact, it flags a *multi-resident contact* and generates
  an appointment.
- **ARP-2, Arrival & Responder Packet.** The merged team's combined form. The top
  sheet has **one signature box**. It is a carbon set. The signature carbons through
  to two pages underneath:
  - **R-1 section: Registration of Arrival.** Name, date, witness, and a field
    called *Body record*, which on Ari's copy is empty and has never been filled.
  - **RA-3 section: Appointment of Community Responder (Sole).** Sole designated
    responder for all 42 households. *Availability: continuous.* Paid £9.60 per hour
    of "active response time". Accommodation: Office G/1, charge applies.
    Commencement 06:00. *The Overnight Support Service will cease on commencement.*
- **R-1 (standalone).** The old single form for registering an arrival. Still legal.
  Nobody uses it because the system has no screen for it. There are forty in a box
  in the lobby cabinet. The guidance note stapled to the box quotes the Arrivals Act
  1952, s.4: *Registration of an arrival shall not be conditional upon any offer of
  employment.*
- **The appointment slip** in the bag was posted to "ARI, Office G/1". It reads:
  *ARRIVAL REGISTRATION & RESPONDER INDUCTION. 05:30, Ferrier Court Lobby. Please
  bring this slip and one witness.*

## 3. Rules of the impossible (obeyed everywhere)

1. **An unanswered address can acquire a presence.** When enough people direct
   real requests at the same absent person through a switched line, something
   begins to answer. Nobody agrees why. Inez: "I don't know what it is. I know
   which relays click." Jad: "It's a clerical error with feelings." Sal: "There's a
   leaflet." Teodor thinks of it as being called, like a vocation. Dima does not
   care. No one gives a lecture and nothing turns out to be a computer.
2. **Anchor.** A presence is anchored to the line the requests were addressed to.
   Ari is anchored to ext 247.
3. **Hearing.** On a call, Ari hears what the other handset's microphone hears. Ari
   can dial extensions and use exchange functions like any other phone (door release,
   hold, conference, transfer).
4. **Borrowing a view.** During a call, with the person's consent, Ari sees from
   where they are and feels some of what their skin feels (warm water, cold, a
   headache's pressure, tiredness). Ari never gets their thoughts or memories and
   never controls their body. The lender feels it as someone reading over their
   shoulder, a warmth at the back of the neck. They can end it at any moment.
5. **Drift.** With no call open and nothing holding it, a presence loses time. Ari
   hangs up at 00:07 and finds it is 00:19. Nothing hurts. Minutes are just gone.
6. **Hold.** The exchange's hold circuit keeps a presence continuous between calls.
   It needs a steady signal to run: the old hold tape, or any steady sound. In the
   old days arrivals waited on hold for the Receiving Room, sometimes for days.
   Inez repairs it in Chapter 3. From then on Ari does not drift.
7. **Receiving.** A presence can be given a body in the Receiving Room if:
   the presence is routed to ext 201; the brass receiving token is in the meter
   (it opens the warm feed to the cradle); and **somebody stays on the Receiving
   handset and keeps talking to them for about twenty minutes**. That is "holding
   the place". If the holder stops, arrival stops safely and the presence returns
   to the line. The body is a new adult human body. It is hungry, it gets cold, it
   will need sleep. It does not take the shape of anyone's wishes. Arrivals keep an
   ear for the line for a few weeks, then it fades and a phone is only a phone.
8. **Registration is separate from receiving and separate from employment.** A body
   without registration is a person without records (no tenancy, no GP, no work).
   Registration needs a clerk and a witness.
9. **A presence can stay on the line indefinitely** if the hold circuit runs and the
   exchange is maintained. It can take calls and borrow views with consent. It has
   no privacy that the exchange does not give it and depends on a 1950s frame and a
   68-year-old woman.

## 4. What actually happened

- **1951 to 1979.** About 140 arrivals received at the Baths. **1964 to 1966:** eleven
  arrivals were registered "& appointed" as Municipal Attendants straight out of the
  cradle, because the people who had called them up had been ringing the Baths
  asking for "the attendant". The council photographed them in identical caps. Look
  closely and they are different men.
- **Eleven weeks ago** the council announced the closure of the Overnight Support
  Service under the DPC scheme.
- **Seven weeks ago** Jad ran a "form clinic" at a table in the lobby over two
  Saturdays. He works at Aldine Copy & Print and is the person the building asks
  when a document needs fixing. For residents who had nobody available, or who had
  people who loved them but could not promise to come at 3am, he wrote **ARI**, the
  name from the sample. He asked Inez to give it a real number that rang somewhere in
  the building. Inez routed 01632 960 247 to G/1 and added the voicemail board.
  Jad thought he was buying time for an appeal. Nobody organised an appeal,
  including him.
- **Nine forms name Ari.** On screen: Mrs. Kaye (2C), Teodor (5A), Nell (3B), and Jad
  himself (4B). Off screen: the Adeyemis (1D), June Pike (1B), Kaveh Rostami (2A),
  Stefan Rusu (6A), Mr. Hollis (6C). Motives differ:
  - **Mrs. Kaye** was afraid of losing practical help. Dima used to take her to
    appointments and is moving. Jad told her "someone will pick up". She heard what
    she needed to and has pictured a young woman from the council with short fair
    hair and red glasses.
  - **Teodor** would not admit his son had stopped answering. Mikael (38) has not
    picked up in four years. Teodor said something at Mikael's wedding, about the
    bride, into a microphone. He pictures Ari as a tall young man in a brown coat,
    which is to say Mikael at nineteen.
  - **Nell** needed the form accepted before the office shut. Her housing support
    plan was marked incomplete without it. She knew Ari was invented. She saved the
    number as "Fake Ari lol". She still rang it at 2am when Tobi had a fever,
    because she "didn't want to wake anyone real".
  - **Jad** put Ari on his own form. His mother and sister are in Montreal and he does
    not ask people for things. He has rung 247 himself, twice, late.
- **Six weeks ago** the forms were submitted. Messages to 247 began. People who knew
  Ari was fictional called anyway because speaking into the receiver helped.
- **Four weeks ago** the system flagged Ari (no record: possible arrival; nine
  nominations: multi-resident contact) and generated the ARP-2 packet and the
  appointment slip, posted to G/1. Nell sorts the lobby post and kept it.
- **Two weeks ago** Inez noticed 247's relays working with no call on the line.
  She said nothing.
- **Three days ago** a council notice went up in the lobby: *Your Community
  Responder, ARI, begins duties at 06:00 on Saturday.* Residents made up a welcome
  bag at the lobby desk. Jad printed "ARI" on his label maker and stuck it on the
  handle.
- **Tonight, 22:40.** Sal Brannock, on the last shift, mentioned to Jad that the
  responder was due at 05:30 and asked if he knew them. Jad said he'd make sure
  they got the bag, took it, and went outside to ring 247 somewhere private. At
  23:00 the council reprogrammed the door fobs for the closure. His stopped working.
  The intercom has been broken since August. At 23:09 he rang 247 and hung up. At
  23:14 he rang again and **somebody picked up**. That is Ari's first memory.

**Ari is new.** There was no earlier Ari whose body was stolen, no amnesia, no
simulation. Ari's earliest impressions are borrowed from the requests: a coat
somebody imagined, a way of being greeted, blue light under water. Ari's
experiences tonight are real and Ari's origin does not make Ari less of a person.

**The second half of the reveal:** the "registration" that looks like the last step
in finding out where Ari belongs would, through the carbon set, appoint Ari as the
sole replacement for the overnight service. The institution has taken the
residents' invented contact as proof that public provision can end.

## 7. Chapter plan

**1. The bag (23:14 to 00:05).** Incoming call. Jad outside, locked out, with a bag.
Teach answer, reply, borrow a view (Jad's hand in the bag, a split beside his
thumbnail). Ari dials 200 and 9 to let him in and hears the street twice. The
lobby, the closing notice, the fluorescent tube (preference). Jad is good company
on the way to G/1. G/1 has been prepared. Jad picks up the desk phone and Ari hears
their own voice come out of it a moment late. The dark window shows Jad, not Ari.
Teodor at the door: "Tall lad. Dark." Mrs. Kaye's text: "red glasses, short fair
hair". Two confident, incompatible descriptions. Jad: "I don't know. I've never
seen you."

**2. People waiting (00:05 to 01:40).** Jad goes upstairs. Ari learns about drift.
The building cutaway becomes a map of who's awake. Dima's call: folding laundry, the
phone on the sill facing the window, the man opposite trying to get a standard lamp
through a doorway with the shade on. Chillies (the funny conversation). Mrs. Kaye's
Thursday: the closure arrives as an inconvenience. Dima asks Ari to look away while
they change (boundary). Nell: a moth, Tobi's drawing, her DPC-1 with Ari on it.
Sal's scripted call and the leaflet on arrivals. Jad's unannounced kindness (the
tube). Inez rings from the frame: "Nothing's plugged into 247." The pool lights.

**3. The receiving room (01:40 to 03:10).** The exchange. The figure and Inez's
routine. The hold circuit and the hold tape (the phrase, unfinished; preference for
what plays). Inez asks Ari to get out of her eyes (boundary). The Receiving Room:
fixtures, photographs, register, repair notes, the token, a three-second
demonstration ("For three seconds I had an elbow"). Teodor rings from G/1: the room
that felt kind in Chapter 1 is arranged like his son's room; the figure in the pool
in Chapter 1 was Teodor doing lengths. "Do you still play?" Jad at the copy shop
at 2am: stacks of near-identical forms, "six weeks", recognising the format. The
disagreement (he's right about not signing things unread; he has another reason).
The voicemail archive is restored. Sal: nine forms, one handwriting, meeting at
half three.

**4. Everybody put your name down (03:10 to 04:30).** Jad confesses first, on the
camp bed in G/1. Ask, rage, leave, or postpone. The original messages are all
there to check. The meeting: chairs around the dry pool, the phone on the chair in
the deep end, everyone facing it. The collage of expectations. Jad tells the
residents himself. Sal lays out the packet; lifting the top sheet shows the carbon
underneath. Sal sends a photo of the old R-1 and s.4. Aftermath: Nell asks how Ari
wants to be saved in her phone. Inez lays out the options plainly.

**5. Morning arrangements (04:30 to 06:30).** Conversations and decisions: body or
not; appointment or not; who holds the place; who takes Mrs. Kaye on Thursday; who
has been told; which papers are kept; whether Jad prints the letter (he asks). The
arrival, if chosen, is the one big perspective rupture: Ari's own eyes, low
resolution, cold, the coat. Sal registers what Ari chooses and catches or misses
the 06:12. The service closes. A minute of the chosen future. Someone calls because
the fog has come in and wants Ari to see it. Something small and tomorrow. The
Casio.

## 10. Endings

**A. Accept the appointment (body + RA-3).** Wages, G/1, useful work, people Ari may
want to help. The schedule is on the wall. Tone is decided by facts, not by the
choice itself:
- *Limits struck* (Ari asked Sal to cross out "continuous"; Sal will only initial
  it if a residents' rota exists and the letter or the meeting put the forms on
  record).
- *Rota* (who agreed to share: Nell, Teodor, Jad if staying, Inez for the frame).
- *Jad recognises Ari's right to stop* (only if he asked what Ari wanted and took
  the answer).
Workable and affectionate with all three; uneasy with some; with none, the phone
doesn't stop and someone says "while you're here".

**B. Register under a chosen name (body + standalone R-1, RA-3 refused).** The
difficulty is the pressure around refusal. The residents must challenge the
closure (stronger if Jad's letter exists) or arrange help themselves. Nell
understands at once; Mrs. Kaye is hurt; Teodor catches himself asking for a favour
and stops. Ari may stay in G/1 for now (Inez: "the council doesn't know it exists")
or not. Refusal is not proof Ari lacks compassion.

**C. Keep the line shared (no body yet, registration deferred).** Needs Inez's
explicit terms: hold running on the sound Ari chose; calls between eight and eleven
unless someone is in trouble; nobody obliged to lend their eyes; Ari can refuse too;
Inez checks the frame Mondays and Thursdays and has written down how, for whoever
comes after her. Precarious, odd, not self-denial.

**Every ending:** a consequence shown as a scene; one intimate follow-up; someone
rings because the fog has come in and they want Ari to see it (Jad if reconciled;
otherwise Inez if trust held; otherwise Dima if trust held; otherwise Teodor;
otherwise Nell, who is always there); a small last exchange that can end on
"tomorrow"; and the Casio, which nobody needs Ari to play.

## 12. Sound

Relays, a fluorescent tube, water in dead pipes, the lift motor through the wall,
fabric on a microphone, shoes on tile, a distant television, breath through a cheap
receiver. Calls are filtered by where the other person is (tiled pool hall, small
flat, street, copy shop, bakery). Music is rare. Every sound that carries
information has a caption.
